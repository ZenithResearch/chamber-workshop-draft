Evaluation — finding weaknesses and checking improvements

The problem: The business needs to discover where models struggle, identify whether the model or operating environment needs changing, and determine whether those changes improve performance.

  • Build evaluation cases around each user’s responsibilities, working context, intended results and observed failures.
  • Define the intended result and acceptance criteria for each function. Assess individual outputs and the complete process.
  • Consider correctness, usefulness, time, cost, rework and adherence to permissions.
  • Capture failures and reviewed corrections with enough context to investigate what needs to change: the model, information, instructions, tools, rules or assignment of work.
  • Compare proposed changes with the current version under comparable conditions, using reviewed examples and execution evidence.
  • Keep evaluation cases separate from examples used for training or tuning, and check whether improvements introduce regressions elsewhere.
  • Identify what can be checked automatically and what requires human judgment. Retain evaluation history to inform adoption decisions.
