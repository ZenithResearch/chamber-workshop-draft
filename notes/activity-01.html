Activity outline

Our purpose

Our mission is to level the informational and technological playing fields.

We’re entering an age where small businesses—and individuals—can stand on the same technological foundations as the world’s largest corporations.

Our responsibility is to help make that possibility practical: the ability to build, improve and collaborate while retaining sovereignty over what you create and the ability to choose who you share and build with.

Adoption — opportunities and risks

Consider where AI could help the business and what manageable adoption would require.

  • What useful result could AI help achieve?
  • What would need to change in the way people work?
  • What information, permissions and review would the work require?
  • Testing, investigating further, postponing adoption or rejecting an unsuitable approach are all useful decisions.

Data strategy and foundations

People repeatedly reconcile applications, find missing information and explain context before work can begin.

  • Define shared structures for describing tasks, information, responsibilities and relationships.
  • Let each person develop an environment suited to their responsibilities.
  • Use clear interfaces and access rules to coordinate with other environments and shared resources.

Sovereignty and competitive advantage

Show the choices a business should be able to make about its knowledge, how that knowledge is used, and where the resulting value accumulates.

The audience should take away that sovereignty preserves practical options: retain, share, license, develop, cooperate and change providers.

Supporting source

OECD: Enhancing Access to and Sharing of Data is a proposed source for the discussion of access, sharing, reuse and associated rights. Specific passages and contextual notes remain to be selected.

Reliability and verifiability

Define what a good result looks like and how it can be inspected.

  • Make responsibilities, permissions and approval points explicit.
  • Keep evidence of the information, rules, decisions and actions behind a result.
  • Evaluate models and working environments to discover weaknesses and assess changes.
  • Distinguish permissions specified in a model from controls enforced by software.

View slide

Operational efficiency

Consider the effort required to reach an accepted result.

  • Look at time, cost, delays, review effort and rework.
  • Choose deliberately what people, established rules, software and AI should do.
  • Retain reviewed corrections so they can become improvements.
  • Consider how capacity can grow while keeping responsibilities and review manageable.

View slide

Orientation

Start with a recurring task from your own business.

  • Use one prepared SOP; rough notes are enough to begin.
  • Build a small instance of the structures introduced in Foundations.
  • Identify people and agent roles, information boundaries, permissions and review criteria.
  • Aim to leave with a useful business decision and a practical next step.
