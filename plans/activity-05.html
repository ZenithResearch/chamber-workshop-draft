ZENITH

Looking Forward

Summary

Explore how verifiable services, shared technical foundations and collaboration could create lasting business value. Identify where these ideas could help participants’ organizations.

Business relevance

Reduce duplicated effort, retain operating knowledge, make service commitments more dependable and cooperate while preserving business independence.

Intended outcome

Participants identify a priority activity to improve, its expected business value and a practical next step.

Bulleted plan

  • Formal Method SLA — clearer service commitments
    • Explore connecting service commitments to precisely stated properties, verification evidence and operating assumptions.
    • Machine interoperability: Explore how shared, machine-readable service specifications and SLA terms could let different tools and agents exchange requests, interpret results and check verification evidence against the same commitments.
    • Discuss what a provider promises, what can be checked and who remains accountable.
    • Connect this to confidence in delegating work and evaluating suppliers.
  • Technology Commons — shared foundations, retained value
    • Introduce jointly developing and maintaining reusable tools, specifications and infrastructure.
    • Consider what businesses could share while retaining control of their information, specialized knowledge and decisions.
    • Connect shared investment to reduced duplication and access to capabilities.
  • Trustless vs Consortium — choosing how to establish trust
    • Compare arrangements that minimize reliance on trusted parties with arrangements governed by identified participants.
    • Consider what must be trusted, what can be verified and how responsibility and disputes are handled.
    • Connect these choices to practical business relationships and coordination costs.
  • Super-collaboration vs Competition — exploring the opportunity
    • Present the working hypothesis that lowering coordination costs and making useful work reusable could create advantages over repeatedly developing capabilities in isolation.
    • Explore how cooperation on shared foundations can coexist with competition in products, expertise and service.
    • Consider how contributors receive value and how shared foundations are maintained.
  • Five Activities worksheet — locating the value
    • List the five most time-consuming recurring activities in the organization.
    • Record who is involved, frequency, estimated total staff time and sources of effort or delay. Rough estimates are sufficient.
    • Select one activity worth investigating and explain what improvement would matter.
  • Choose a practical next step
    • Consider what to improve internally, obtain as a service or pursue collaboratively.
    • Identify a small experiment or investigation, the support needed and how its value would be assessed.

Evidence of success

Participants leave with five candidate activities, one reasoned priority, an expected benefit and a feasible next step.
